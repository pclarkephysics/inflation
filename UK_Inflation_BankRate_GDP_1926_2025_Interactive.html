How to read it: all three series are percentages, so they can be compared directly. Inflation measures the annual change in prices; Bank Rate is the average official policy rate during the year; GDP growth measures the annual change in real output. A negative inflation value means the RPI fell compared with the previous year (deflation). A negative GDP value means real output contracted. The graph shows the annual percentage change in the UK Retail Prices Index (RPI). RPI is used here because it provides a continuous long-run series suitable for a 100-year view. Modern CPI is the government's headline inflation measure, but CPI is only available as an official National Statistic from 1989; earlier CPI estimates are modelled.
Sources: Office for National Statistics, Retail Prices Index: Long run series: annual percentage change and GDP series IHYP; Bank of England, annual average official Bank Rate series IUAABEDR, series CDSI, GDP series IHYP released 13 August 2026; Bank Rate series IUAABEDR accessed September 2026.
